# Independent verification of the complement-two interval certificates

This note describes the independently written C++ verifiers

* `v4_upper_complement_two_j1_verify.cpp`, for the family j=1,
  ell=k−2 in the upper branch;
* `v4_upper_complement_two_l1_verify.cpp`, for j=i−2, ell=1 in
  both branches, including the extended fixed-top interval file.

The analytic upper-index bounds and the meaning of the retained
intervals are proved in the corresponding mathematical notes. The
verifiers independently check every numerical endpoint and every
congruence witness in the saved binary certificates.

## 1. A different, outward-rounded logarithm enclosure

The generators use scale 2^56, 24 atanh terms, and a proved aggregate
rounding margin. The verifiers instead use S=2^52, 28 terms, and
propagate lower and upper bounds separately at every operation.

For z=a/b in [0,1/3], let

\[
z_- =\lfloor Sz\rfloor,\qquad z_+=\lceil Sz\rceil,
\]

\[
w_-=\lfloor z_-^2/S\rfloor,\qquad
w_+=\lceil z_+^2/S\rceil.
\]

Starting at P_0^-=z_- and P_0^+=z_+, define

\[
P_{n+1}^-=\lfloor P_n^-w_-/S\rfloor,\qquad
P_{n+1}^+=\lceil P_n^+w_+/S\rceil.
\]

Induction gives P_n^-/S≤z^(2n+1)≤P_n^+/S. Each term is divided
by 2n+1 with the same outward rounding direction, then the sums are
multiplied by 2. The omitted tail satisfies

\[
0\le2\sum_{n=28}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{2n+1}
\le\frac9{4\cdot57\cdot3^{57}}<\frac1S.
\]

The last inequality is checked as an exact integer comparison in the
verifier. Adding one unit to the upper sum therefore encloses
S·2atanh(z), independently of the generator's rounding analysis.

As before, integer n is normalized as n=2^e times a number in [1,2).
The identity

\[
\log n=e\log2+2\operatorname{atanh}\frac{n-2^e}{n+2^e}
\]

produces a rigorous interval for S log(n). All normalization and
square-root operations in the verifier are integer operations.

## 2. Separate binomial intervals

For each binomial coefficient, the verifier encloses

\[
2S\left[\log\binom nr+\tfrac12\log(2\pi)\right]
\]

using the Robbins factorial remainder and the numerator-product
remainder inequalities proved in
`v4_upper_complement_two_j1_certification.md`. Subtracting the two
independently enclosed quantities cancels the common constant.

For the j=1 family it compares C(B+t,t) with C(k²,4k−4).
For the fixed-top family it compares C(C(i,2),r) with C(i²,4i−4).
The lower endpoint is accepted only if the entire logarithm-difference
interval lies below 0. The upper endpoint is accepted only if it lies
above an upper bound for log(2).

Together with the monotonicity and X<left<2X or Y<left<2Y condition
proved in the family notes, these endpoints exclude all omitted
integers. For indices below 1024, the complete necessary interval is
retained and its endpoints must exactly equal that interval's bounds.

## 3. Complete binary coverage and independent Lucas calculations

The verifiers check the magic header, every consecutive upper index,
every interval endpoint, all witness bytes, and the exact end of file.
No candidate may be silently skipped.

The increasing prime list is generated by a sieve, and the small
binomial coefficients used in Lucas's theorem are generated by Pascal's
recurrence. This differs from the generators' factorial/inverse tables.
Every recorded original equation must have unequal residues at its
supplied prime.

For the fixed-top family, each retained r has two witness bytes, for
the original values k=r and k=A−r. Coincident branches when A=2r
are recorded separately in the report so that the number of distinct
original equations is also available.

## 4. Arithmetic limits and reproduction

The verifiers use unsigned 128-bit intermediates and signed 128-bit
interval endpoints. Even in the extended i<2^21 run, the largest
Taylor-remainder numerator is below

\[
2(5\cdot2^{21})^3\,2^{52}=250\cdot2^{115}<2^{123}.
\]

Products of logarithm enclosures and their coefficients, denominators,
and interval sums are smaller. The binomial integer arguments are
below 2^42. Thus all stated ranges fit the chosen types.

Compile without disabling assertions:

```bash
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 v4_upper_complement_two_j1_verify.cpp -o verify_j1
./verify_j1
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 v4_upper_complement_two_l1_verify.cpp -o verify_l1
./verify_l1 v4_upper_complement_two_l1_extended.bin v4_upper_complement_two_l1_extended_verification.json
```

No floating-point operations occur in these verifiers. Their reports
record the checked ranges and counts. These are exact-integer
computer-assisted certificates, not Lean proofs of the interval
analysis or of the complete original Diophantine problem.
